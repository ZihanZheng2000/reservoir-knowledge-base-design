## SC-LP-001 — Drought-severity forecasts translate into the 2026 Glen Canyon Dam release decision

- Primary pattern: decision_process
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: How do Lake Powell inflow forecasts and elevation-tier classification, through the Interim Guidelines' four-tier structure and the DROA, translate into an annual Glen Canyon Dam release decision such as the 2026 reduction?
- Synthesis claim: A near-record-low WY2026 inflow forecast was classified through the CRSS/24-Month Study process into the Mid-Elevation Release Tier band, which triggered the 2024 SEIS ROD's Section 6.E emergency-reduction authority once the minimum-probable study projected Lake Powell below 3,500 ft; Interior then set the WY2026 release at 6.00 maf (down from the tier's default 7.48 maf) and paired it with a DROA-triggered supplemental release from Flaming Gorge Reservoir, publicly announced through Reclamation's April 2026 newsroom release.
- Scope and conditions: Water Year 2026 Glen Canyon Dam operations, as reflected in the Draft 2026 Annual Operating Plan and the July 2026 Most Probable 24-Month Study; the Draft AOP is not yet a finalized document.

### Analysis chain

1. **hydrologic forecast signal** — WY2026 unregulated inflow was forecast near-record-low: 3.50 maf (36% of the 1991-2020 average) in the July 2026 Most Probable 24-Month Study, with the April 2026 minimum-probable case at 2.78 maf (29% of average), one of the lowest on record. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-023-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-024-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-035-01`, `EU-LP-MED-001-05`.
2. **tier/model classification** — Running this forecast through the 24-Month Study/CRSS workflow places Lake Powell within the Mid-Elevation Release Tier band (elevation 3,525-3,575 ft) and flags the DROA's 3,525 ft target elevation as at risk, with the April study additionally projecting a decline below the 3,490 ft minimum power pool absent intervention. 4. **operational choice** — The Department of the Interior implemented Section 6.E to reduce Lake Powell's WY2026 annual release from 7.48 maf to 6.00 maf, and paired this with a DROA-triggered 660,000 acre-foot to 1 maf supplemental release from Flaming Gorge Reservoir into Lake Powell (April 2026-April 2027), agreed to by the four Upper Basin states. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-023-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-035-02`, `EU-LP-MED-001-02`.
5. **implementation and public record** — Reclamation announced and documented the combined release-reduction and Flaming-Gorge-release package through its April 2026 newsroom release and the Draft 2026 Annual Operating Plan, framing it as an unprecedented action to protect Glen Canyon Dam's water-delivery and hydropower infrastructure. Evidence: `EU-LP-MED-001-01`, `EU-LP-OFF-035-02`.

## SC-LP-002 — Tributary sediment monitoring translates into an HFE release decision

- Primary pattern: decision_process
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: How does tributary sediment input, monitored and modeled as a sand-mass balance, translate into a High-Flow Experiment (HFE) release decision at Glen Canyon Dam, and what can override an otherwise-triggered release?
- Synthesis claim: Since 2012, Paria River tributary sand inputs are tracked and used to run a fall sand-mass-balance accounting process (July 1-November 30) that determines HFE eligibility; the formal 2012 protocol and 2016 LTEMP ROD translate that sediment signal into a release of up to ~45,000 cfs combining full powerplant and bypass-tube capacity, but the same ROD lets the legal requirement to meet the annual release volume override an otherwise-eligible extended-duration HFE, and post-release monitoring feeds back into the program's understanding of protocol effectiveness.
- Scope and conditions: Applies to Glen Canyon Dam HFEs conducted under the post-2012 protocol and the 2016 LTEMP ROD (Alternative D); does not apply to the pre-2012 experimental flows discussed in SC-LP-013.

### Analysis chain

1. **sediment monitoring/trigger basis** — Paria River tributary sand inputs are monitored and modeled as the sand-mass-balance basis for HFE eligibility, with a 10-year protocol implementing one to two HFEs annually timed to follow fall and/or spring sand inputs. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-018-01`, `EU-LP-RES-014-05`.
2. **formal protocol rule** — The 2012 DOI-approved protocol coordinates dam releases to follow large tributary sand inputs; under LTEMP Alternative D, extended-duration (greater than 96-hour) fall HFEs are capped at no more than four occurrences over the 20-year LTEMP period, triggered by the modeled sand-mass balance for the July 1-November 30 accounting period. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-018-01`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-10`.
3. **legal override check** — An extended-duration HFE will not be implemented if doing so would affect the annual release volume, meaning the legal requirement to meet that year's annual release volume can override an otherwise sediment-triggered extended-duration HFE. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-003-11`.
4. **release execution** — An HFE release is defined as using full powerplant capacity (about 30,000 cfs) plus the four river-outlet-works bypass tubes (about 15,000 cfs), for a combined magnitude up to about 45,000 cfs, with durations of about 2-7 days (most standard HFEs capped at 96 hours). Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-004-07`, `EU-LP-OFF-007-05`, `EU-LP-RES-024-10`.
5. **monitored outcome feeding back** — The 45-site long-term sandbar monitoring network, together with daily repeat photography and annual surveys for 2012-2018 HFEs, measures post-HFE sandbar response, informing the program's evolving understanding of protocol effectiveness. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-005-20`, `EU-LP-RES-018-14`, `EU-LP-RES-018-15`.

## SC-LP-003 — Physical, elevation-graded, and reliability constraints jointly define release capacity below minimum power pool

- Primary pattern: constraint_structure
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: As Lake Powell's elevation declines toward and below minimum power pool (3,490 ft), what jointly binding physical, elevation-graded, and reliability constraints define the feasible Glen Canyon Dam release volume, and how much flexibility remains?
- Synthesis claim: Below elevation 3,490 ft, Glen Canyon Dam's release path narrows from eight penstocks to only the river outlet works (ROW), whose own per-tube capacity is further reduced by cavitation limits as elevation falls; Reclamation's own 2024 assessment additionally concludes the ROW cannot be reliably used as a sole long-term release path, while the Law of the River's 8.23 maf/year minimum objective release remains legally in force regardless of this shrinking physical capacity -- together defining why Reclamation treats 3,490 ft (and the 3,525 ft DROA buffer above it) as hard operating priorities rather than discretionary targets.
- Scope and conditions: Applies to Glen Canyon Dam operations as Lake Powell elevation approaches or falls below 3,490 ft (minimum power pool); figures reflect 2024 interim operating guidance and pre-existing design documentation.

### Analysis chain

1. **physical release-path constraint** — Above 3,490 ft, releases pass through eight penstocks; below 3,490 ft the powerplant cannot be used at all, leaving only the four river-outlet-works tubes, physically limited to about 15,000 cfs total at unrestricted design capacity. Evidence: `EU-LP-CRS-002-03`, `EU-LP-MED-002-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-030-02`.
2. **elevation-graded capacity constraint** — ROW per-tube capacity is itself cavitation-limited and steps down further as elevation drops, from about 3,185 cfs per tube at 3,490 ft to 2,200 cfs at 3,420 ft, 1,200 cfs at 3,400 ft, and 0 cfs at 3,390 ft under the 2024 interim operating guidance. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-025-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-027-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-007-06`.
3. **reliability constraint** — Reclamation's 2024 Technical Decision Memorandum concludes the ROW were never designed for sustained long-term conveyance, documenting a 1965 tailrace failure and 2023 cavitation damage as evidence the ROW should not be relied upon as the sole release path. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-025-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-06`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-07`, `EU-LP-MED-002-03`.
4. **legal delivery constraint** — The Law of the River's 8.23 maf/year minimum objective release remains in force regardless of physical capacity; a low-elevation, ROW-limited scenario (e.g., only 7.41 maf/year available at elevation 3,430 ft) would fall well short of this obligation. Evidence: `EU-LP-CRS-002-08`, `EU-LP-OFF-005-14`.
5. **resulting feasible operating space** — The institutional response is to operate to keep Lake Powell at or above 3,490 ft (and ideally above the 3,525 ft DROA buffer) rather than test a sustained ROW-only regime, since dead pool at 3,370 ft would leave no controlled release path at all. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-025-01`, `EU-LP-OFF-031-01`, `EU-LP-MED-002-04`, `EU-LP-RES-013-04`.

## SC-LP-004 — Safety, sediment, and power-flexibility constraints jointly define the MLFF/LTEMP hourly-release envelope

- Primary pattern: constraint_structure
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: What jointly relevant safety/recreation, sediment/ecosystem, and power-system-flexibility constraints define Glen Canyon Dam's allowable hourly and daily release pattern under MLFF and LTEMP Alternative D?
- Synthesis claim: The 1996 ROD set a baseline safety/recreation-driven envelope (minimum/maximum release, ramp rates, and a 3-tier daily-fluctuation cap) grounded in a 1995 river-guide/public preference study; the 2016 LTEMP ROD then revised the fluctuation formula and down-ramp rate specifically to reduce sandbar erosion, while WAPA continues to exercise discretionary hourly/weekly dispatch choices inside these limits that it can alter without a new NEPA/ROD process, leaving the up-ramp rate the one figure unchanged since 1996.
- Scope and conditions: Applies to Glen Canyon Dam's sub-annual (hourly/daily/monthly) release pattern under MLFF (1996-2016) and LTEMP Alternative D (2016-present); does not govern total annual release volume (see KC-LP-REG-05 / SC-LP-006).

### Analysis chain

1. **baseline safety/recreation constraint** — The 1996 ROD set a minimum release of 8,000 cfs (day) / 5,000 cfs (night), a maximum release of 25,000 cfs, an up-ramp limit of 4,000 cfs/hour, a down-ramp limit of 1,500 cfs/hour, and a 3-tier daily-fluctuation cap topping out at 8,000 cfs/day, based on a 1995 study of river-guide and public flow preferences. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-008-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-06`, `EU-LP-OFF-006-02`.
2. **sediment/ecosystem-driven revision** — LTEMP Alternative D (2016 ROD) replaced the fixed 3-tier fluctuation schedule with a formula proportional to that month's release volume (9-10x monthly volume in kaf) and raised the down-ramp limit to 2,500 cfs/hour, changes explicitly framed as reducing sandbar erosion rates. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-003-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-05`, `EU-LP-RES-018-04`.
3. **power-flexibility constraint operating within the above** — WAPA's discretionary hourly and weekly dispatch practice -- maximizing releases during peak-demand periods (Monday-Saturday, 7 a.m.-11 p.m.) and keeping weekend releases at no less than 85 percent of average weekday release -- operates inside these formal limits and can be altered by WAPA at any time without a new ROD/EIS process. ## SC-LP-007 — Warm-water releases benefiting humpback chub simultaneously raise nonnative-fish predation risk to the same species

- Primary pattern: operational_tradeoff
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: How does using warmer Glen Canyon Dam releases to benefit humpback chub simultaneously create nonnative-fish predation and competition risk to that same species, and what flow-based countermeasures has Reclamation used?
- Synthesis claim: Reduced Lake Powell storage during drought warms Glen Canyon Dam releases in a way intended to benefit native humpback chub, but the same warming is modeled to also benefit nonnative fish, raising predation and competition pressure on humpback chub; Reclamation has responded with targeted suppression and management flows (2003-2005 fluctuating suppression releases, and Trout Management Flows under LTEMP Alternative D) and has institutionally committed to monitor and control nonnative fish if drought-driven warming recurs.
- Scope and conditions: Applies to Glen Canyon Dam release-temperature effects on humpback chub and nonnative fish (principally rainbow trout) under drought-driven low-storage conditions, as documented for the 2003-2011 and 2014 warming episodes and the LTEMP Alternative D management-flow tools.

### Analysis chain

1. **ecological objective/mechanism** — River warming that occurred from 2003 to 2011 (and again in 2014), resulting from reduced Lake Powell storage, was intended to benefit native humpback chub reproduction and growth. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-014-06`.
2. **modeled side effect** — The Grand Canyon Ecosystem Model predicted that nonnative fish would also benefit from the same warming, potentially increasing predation and competition risk to humpback chub through greater fish interactions in critical river segments. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-014-06`.
