## 3. The governing operating framework

Lake Powell's releases are governed by a layered set of legal and technical instruments built up since 1922. The 1922 Colorado River Compact apportioned 7.5 million acre-feet (maf) annually to each of the Upper and Lower Basins (`KC-LP-REG-01`), and the Colorado River Storage Project Act of 1956 authorized Glen Canyon Dam for regulation of Colorado River flow, storage for beneficial consumptive use, enabling Upper Basin Compact apportionments, flood control, and incidental hydropower generation (`KC-LP-REG-03`). Since 1970, the Criteria for Coordinated Long-Range Operation of Colorado River Reservoirs (LROC) has set a minimum objective annual release of 8.23 maf from Lake Powell, implemented annually through the Annual Operating Plan under the Colorado River Basin Project Act of 1968 (`KC-LP-OPR-08`, `KC-LP-REG-06`, `KC-LP-REG-07`).

The 2007 Interim Guidelines layer a four-tier, elevation-based coordinated-operations structure on top of this baseline — the Equalization Tier, Upper Elevation Balancing Tier, Mid-Elevation Release Tier, and Lower Elevation Balancing Tier — with Lake Powell's scheduled release adjusted monthly based on forecast inflow and projected September 30 elevations at both Lake Powell and Lake Mead (`KC-LP-OPR-11`). Below the Mid-Elevation Release Tier's range (elevation 3,525-3,575 ft), the Lower Elevation Balancing Tier sets a release range of 7.0 to 9.5 maf (`KC-LP-OPR-14`), while a matching Lower Basin shortage-tier structure governs Lake Mead deliveries, curtailing Arizona and Nevada water when Lake Mead falls below elevation 1,075 feet (`KC-LP-OPR-10`).

Since the 1996 Record of Decision, Glen Canyon Dam's sub-annual (hourly/daily) release pattern has been governed separately by the Modified Low Fluctuating Flow (MLFF) regime, which replaced historic hydroelectric peaking operations specifically to reduce daily flow fluctuations and protect downstream resources (`KC-LP-OPR-17`; see Synthesis Card `SC-LP-008` for the 1991-to-1996 regime transition). MLFF set a minimum release of 8,000 cfs (day) / 5,000 cfs (night), a maximum of 25,000 cfs, ramp-rate limits, and a tiered daily-fluctuation cap topping out at 8,000 cfs/day (`KC-LP-OPR-01`). The 2016 LTEMP Record of Decision (Alternative D) revised the fluctuation formula and ramp rates specifically to reduce sandbar erosion (`KC-LP-OPR-03`; `SC-LP-004`), while explicitly leaving total annual release volume outside its scope — that remains governed by LROC and the Interim Guidelines (`KC-LP-REG-05`). This joint constraint structure carries a documented, quantified cost: MLFF's ramp-rate restrictions reduced Western Area Power Administration (WAPA) hydropower flexibility and cost WAPA and its customers an average of $38-50 million per year between 1997 and 2005 (`KC-LP-UNC-07`; `SC-LP-005`).

## 4. Drought response and the 2026 emergency actions

The Colorado River Basin's 2000-2022 period was the driest 23-year stretch in over a century of record (`KC-LP-UNC-03`). This sustained drought produced a chain of increasingly specific drought-response instruments: the Drought Response Operations Agreement (DROA), which established a 3,525 ft "target elevation" at Lake Powell (35 feet above minimum power pool) to sequence response actions before the reservoir approaches minimum power pool (`KC-LP-OPR-20`, `KC-LP-EMR-04`); the 2019 Drought Contingency Plans; and the 2024 near-term consensus agreement's new Section XI.G.6.E authority, which lets the Secretary plan to cut Lake Powell releases to not less than 6.0 maf if the minimum-probable 24-Month Study projects the reservoir below elevation 3,500 ft (`KC-LP-OPR-13`).

Water Year 2026 tested this framework directly. The July 2026 Most Probable 24-Month Study forecast WY2026 unregulated inflow at 3.50 maf (36 percent of the 1991-2020 average), with an April 2026 minimum-probable case of just 2.78 maf (29 percent of average) — one of the lowest on record (`KC-LP-STG-09`, `KC-LP-STG-10`). Reclamation's April 2026 24-Month Study had projected Lake Powell could decline below the 3,490 ft minimum power pool by August 2026 absent intervention. In response, the Department of the Interior invoked Section 6.E to reduce the WY2026 Glen Canyon Dam release from 7.48 maf to 6.00 maf, and, with the consent of the four Upper Basin states, committed to releasing 660,000 acre-feet to 1 maf from Flaming Gorge Reservoir into Lake Powell between April 2026 and April 2027 (`KC-LP-EMR-06`). Synthesis Card `SC-LP-001` traces the full decision chain from forecast to implementation, and `SC-LP-009` frames this package as an emergency regime layered on top of — not replacing — the standing MLFF/Interim Guidelines baseline. Reclamation's own projection is that the combined package will raise Lake Powell's elevation by about 54 feet, to at least 3,500 ft, by April 2027 (`SC-LP-014`); this is an explicitly forward-looking projection, not yet an observed outcome, and should be rechecked against later 24-Month Studies.

Separately, calendar year 2026 also sits under a Level 1 Shortage Condition for Lake Mead (projected elevation 1,055.88 ft), requiring Arizona to contribute 512,000 acre-feet (18 percent of its apportionment), Nevada 21,000 acre-feet (7 percent), and Mexico 80,000 acre-feet (5 percent) (`KC-LP-EMR-07`).

## 5. Infrastructure constraints and reliability risk at low elevation

Glen Canyon Dam's release capacity is not a single number; it is a function of reservoir elevation. Above elevation 3,490 ft (minimum power pool), releases pass through eight penstocks; below it, the powerplant cannot operate at all, and only the river outlet works (ROW) — four 96-inch-diameter steel pipes with a combined design capacity of about 15,000 cfs — remain available (`KC-LP-STG-01`, `KC-LP-EMR-13`). That ROW capacity is itself elevation-graded and cavitation-limited: Reclamation's 2024 interim operating guidance sets per-conduit flow limits stepping down from 3,185 cfs at elevation 3,490 ft to zero at 3,390 ft (`KC-LP-OPR-07`). Synthesis Card `SC-LP-003` connects these physical and elevation-graded constraints to Reclamation's own 2024 conclusion that the ROW should not be relied upon as the dam's sole long-term release mechanism — a conclusion grounded partly in a documented 1965 tailrace-slab failure caused by the ROW performing differently from its design-basis hydraulic model (`KC-LP-UNC-02`; `SC-LP-012`), and partly in 2023 cavitation damage observed during an actual low-reservoir ROW release (`KC-LP-UNC-02`).

Because the Law of the River's 8.23 maf/year minimum objective release remains legally in force regardless of physical capacity, a sustained low-elevation, ROW-limited scenario would fall well short of that obligation — for example, only about 7.41 maf/year would be available at elevation 3,430 ft (`KC-LP-UNC-01`; `SC-LP-003`). This is the underlying physical logic for why Reclamation treats 3,490 ft, and the 3,525 ft DROA buffer above it, as hard operating priorities rather than discretionary targets (Section 4).

Low elevation also carries a distinct water-quality risk, separate from the release-capacity risk above: in September 2005, historically low reservoir elevation placed the reservoir's low-dissolved-oxygen metalimnetic layer at the penstock withdrawal elevation, and oxygen-depleted water passed through the dam, causing tailwater dissolved oxygen to violate applicable EPA cold-water-fishery criteria (`KC-LP-UNC-10`; `SC-LP-011`).

## 6. High-flow experiments, sediment, and ecological flow tools

Since 2012, Glen Canyon Dam's High-Flow Experiment (HFE) protocol times releases of up to about 45,000 cfs to follow measured Paria River tributary sediment inputs, rather than running on a fixed schedule (`KC-LP-EMR-08`, `KC-LP-OPR-04`; `SC-LP-002`). This design followed two earlier (1997 and 2000) sediment-redistribution flow tests that failed to build sandbars for two different documented reasons — insufficient release magnitude in 1997, and insufficient antecedent sediment supply in 2000 (`KC-LP-UNC-11`; `SC-LP-013`). Under the 2016 LTEMP ROD, extended-duration fall HFEs are capped at four occurrences over the 20-year LTEMP period, but the ROD subordinates this ecological tool to the legal annual-release-volume obligation: an eligible extended HFE will not be implemented if it would affect that year's required annual release volume (`KC-LP-OPR-05`; `SC-LP-006`).

The 45-site long-term sandbar monitoring network shows the protocol produced measurable short-term sandbar growth after the 2012-2014 HFEs (52-57 percent of sites larger immediately post-event), but long-term effectiveness diverges structurally by bar type: wide reattachment bars can now at best only replace sediment lost between events due to vegetation-driven channel narrowing, while separation and eddy bars have not been durably rebuilt at all (`KC-LP-MOD-06`, `KC-LP-UNC-12`; `SC-LP-015`).

Temperature- and flow-based ecological tools carry their own tradeoffs. Reduced Lake Powell storage warms Glen Canyon Dam releases in a way intended to benefit native humpback chub, but the same warming is modeled to also benefit nonnative fish, raising predation and competition risk to the same species Reclamation is trying to help — a risk Reclamation has responded to with targeted suppression flows (2003-2005) and Trout Management Flows under LTEMP Alternative D (`KC-LP-UNC-08`, `KC-LP-EMR-10`, `KC-LP-OPR-18`; `SC-LP-007`).

## 7. Governance and the post-2026 transition

Dam operations are advised, but not controlled, by the Glen Canyon Dam Adaptive Management Program (GCDAMP): its Adaptive Management Work Group (AMWG) and associated Structured Decision Analysis process inform, but do not replace, the Secretary of the Interior's sole statutory authority to select operating alternatives (`KC-LP-REG-08`, `KC-LP-REG-09`). The 2007 Interim Guidelines were deliberately designed as a 20-year interim framework and are scheduled to expire at the end of 2026, alongside the 2019 Drought Contingency Plans and international Minutes 323/330 — a scheduled expiration that formally triggered Reclamation's Post-2026 NEPA process in 2023 (`KC-LP-REG-02`; `SC-LP-010`). Basin States faced a mid-February 2026 deadline for a final seven-state agreement targeting 2027 implementation; one proposed alternative (Basic Coordination) would continue the existing Lower Elevation Balancing Tier's 7.0-9.5 maf range and 3,525 ft threshold, suggesting at least one path forward is designed for continuity rather than a wholesale rule change (`KC-LP-OPR-14`; `SC-LP-010`).

## 8. Uncertainty, contested figures, and unresolved issues

This knowledge base preserves several unresolved discrepancies rather than silently reconciling them, consistent with the project's conservative consolidation and synthesis rules:

- **Powerplant discharge capacity** is reported at both approximately 31,500 cfs and approximately 33,000-33,200 cfs across different sources, with no source explaining the difference (design/nameplate vs. as-built/operational capacity is one plausible explanation, but it is not confirmed by the evidence) (`KC-LP-OPR-15`).
- **2021-2022 DROA upstream release volumes** are reported differently by a CRS report (180,000 AF in 2021) and Reclamation's own DROA page (161,000 AF in 2021), with a similar mismatch for 2022 (`KC-LP-EMR-05`).
- **The Cool Mix Alternative's monitoring location** is given as river mile 61 in the 2024 SEIS and river mile 30 in the 2026 Draft AOP, for what otherwise appears to be the same 15.5°C/three-day trigger (`KC-LP-EMR-15`).
- **The pre-1996-ROD interim operating criteria's establishment date** is given as November 1991 by one source and August 1991 by another (`KC-LP-REG-14`; `SC-LP-008`).
- **CRSS elevation projections** are explicitly based on the 100-year historic hydrologic record rather than published climate-change flow-reduction projections, a modeling limitation independently reaffirmed by two separate Reclamation effects analyses (`KC-LP-MOD-02`).

More broadly, 229 of 442 EUs (52 percent) are not cited by any Knowledge Card; this reflects the project's conservative grouping rule (only consolidate genuine same-question repeats, complements, or contested pairs), not a coverage gap, and those EUs remain available in `evidence_units.jsonl` and the index for direct lookup.
