## SC-LP-001 — How WY2026 inflow forecasts were translated into a two-step cut in Glen Canyon Dam releases

- Primary pattern: decision_process
- Secondary lenses: operating_regime_change
- Operational question: How did Water Year 2026 inflow forecasts, tier classification, and the 2024 Section 6.E authority combine to produce the 2026 reduction in Lake Powell releases?
- Synthesis claim: The WY2026 release decision moved through a documented sequence: the August 2025 24-Month Study placed Lake Powell in the Mid-Elevation Release Tier with a projected 7.48 maf release; projections below the 3,525 ft DROA target elevation led Reclamation to reduce December 2025-April 2026 monthly releases by 0.598 maf; and when continued record-low inflow forecasts projected Lake Powell below 3,500 ft, Interior invoked Section 6.E of the 2024 ROD to cut the annual release from 7.48 to 6.00 maf, paired with 0.66-1.0 maf of DROA releases from Flaming Gorge.
- Scope and conditions: Water Year 2026 operations of Glen Canyon Dam under the 2007 Interim Guidelines as supplemented by the 2024 Near-term Colorado River Operations SEIS ROD. Section 6.E applies only in the Mid-Elevation Release and Lower Elevation Balancing Tiers and is triggered by the minimum probable 24-Month Study; it sets a floor of 6.0 maf. The 0.598 maf monthly adjustment and the 6.00 maf annual figure are reported in the July 2026 24-Month Study; the 1.48 maf reduction is stated by Reclamation as applying through September 2026.

### Analysis chain

1. **hydrologic forecast signal** — CBRFC Ensemble Streamflow Prediction scenarios (probable minimum, most probable, probable maximum) feed the 24-Month Study; by spring-summer 2026 the WY2026 unregulated inflow forecast had fallen to about 3.50 maf (36% of average), with a minimum probable inflow of 2.78 maf (29% of average). Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-022-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-023-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-024-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-035-01`.
2. **tier classification** — The August 2025 24-Month Study projected a January 1, 2026 Lake Powell elevation below 3,575 ft and at or above 3,525 ft, so the Mid-Elevation Release Tier governed WY2026 with a projected 7.48 maf release. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-022-01`, `EU-LP-OFF-023-01`.
3. **first response: protect the DROA target elevation** — The July 2025 24-Month Study projected Lake Powell below the 3,525 ft DROA target elevation, triggering drought-response planning; to protect that target, December 2025-April 2026 monthly releases were reduced by 0.598 maf in the December 2025 24-Month Study. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-022-03`, `EU-LP-OFF-023-03`.
4. **applicable rule** — Section 6.E of the 2024 ROD directs that, in the Mid-Elevation Release or Lower Elevation Balancing Tier, if the minimum probable 24-Month Study projects an elevation below 3,500 ft within 12 months, the Secretary begin planning to reduce releases to not less than 6.0 maf to maintain 3,500 ft. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-002-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-021-10`.
5. **operational decision and implementation** — With conditions projected below 3,500 ft, Interior reduced the WY2026 release from 7.48 to 6.00 maf under Section 6.E, in conjunction with a 2026 Drought Response Operations Plan releasing about 0.66-1.0 maf from Flaming Gorge by April 2027; Reclamation described the combined package as adding up to about 2.48 maf to Lake Powell. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-023-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-035-02`, `EU-LP-MED-001-03`.

## SC-LP-002 — A met sediment trigger is necessary but not sufficient for a High-Flow Experiment

- Primary pattern: decision_process
- Secondary lenses: operational_tradeoff
- Operational question: How is a High-Flow Experiment (HFE) at Glen Canyon Dam decided once a sediment trigger is reached, and what conditions can stop it?
- Synthesis claim: Under the 2012 protocol and LTEMP, a sediment trigger makes an HFE eligible rather than automatic: the decision is then screened against annual-release-volume limits, reservoir elevation, and other resource conditions. Documented outcomes show these screens are used in practice: fall HFEs were not implemented in 2015, 2021, and 2022 although sediment triggers were met, because of warm release temperatures and nonnative-fish entrainment risk, and the 2024 SEIS changed the sediment accounting so that untriggered or forgone sediment can roll over.
- Scope and conditions: HFEs under the 2012 HFE protocol and the 2016 LTEMP ROD (Alternative D), as amended by the 2024 LTEMP SEIS. The extended-duration fall HFE limits (no more than four in 20 years; not implemented if annual release volume would be affected) apply to that HFE type only. The 3,500 ft limit is a modeling assumption stated in the 2024 SEIS, not a codified ROD rule.

### Analysis chain

1. **eligibility signal** — HFEs follow a decision-making protocol approved in 2012 that times dam releases to follow large tributary sand inputs; the protocol defines conditions for implementation and a method for setting timing, magnitude, and duration. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-018-01`, `EU-LP-OFF-005-19`, `EU-LP-OFF-006-12`.
2. **water-delivery and legal screen** — HFEs change only monthly and hourly release patterns while annual volumes continue to comply with the LROC as implemented through the 2007 Interim Guidelines; an extended-duration fall HFE will not be implemented if annual release volume would be affected, and no more than four are allowed over the 20-year LTEMP period. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-006-10`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-11`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-10`.
3. **reservoir-elevation screen** — The 2024 SEIS assumes no HFE below Lake Powell elevation 3,500 ft, because achievable magnitude would fall below 37,000 cfs and a release could increase the risk of dropping below the 3,490 ft power pool. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-007-08`.
4. **other-resource screen and documented outcomes** — The Department decided not to implement fall HFEs in 2015, 2021, and 2022 despite reaching sediment input triggers, citing increased water temperatures and higher potential for entrainment of warmwater nonnative fish. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-007-07`.
5. **procedural requirement and protocol revision** — Traditionally Associated Tribes must be notified at least 30 days before planned experimental flows including HFEs; the 2024 SEIS replaced the semiannual sediment accounting period with an annual period allowing a spring or fall HFE, or both, with rollover of sediment when a trigger is met but no HFE is implemented. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-003-20`, `EU-LP-OFF-007-14`.

## SC-LP-003 — The annual Lake Powell release is set once a year and then recomputed monthly

- Primary pattern: decision_process
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: Through what process is Lake Powell's annual release volume determined and adjusted during a water year?
- Synthesis claim: The annual release is not a single fixed decision. The LROC is implemented each year through the Annual Operating Plan (AOP) using the 2007 Interim Guidelines; the 24-Month Study projects storage and sets the operating tier from the projected end-of-calendar-year (January 1) Lake Powell elevation; and in the Equalization and Balancing Tiers the scheduled water-year release is adjusted each month as inflow forecasts and projected September 30 storage change. Basin States are given the 24-Month Study assumptions before the April and August studies that drive these adjustments.
- Scope and conditions: Annual release determination for Glen Canyon Dam under the LROC, the 2007 Interim Guidelines (through 2026), and the 2019 DCP Companion Agreement. Monthly adjustment as described here applies in the Equalization, Upper Elevation Balancing, and Lower Elevation Balancing Tiers; the Mid-Elevation Release Tier specifies a fixed volume (7.48 maf) subject to later rules such as Section 6.E. The January-July monthly recomputation description comes from a 1996 GAO report and predates the 2007 tiers.

### Analysis chain

1. **legal implementation mechanism** — The Interim Guidelines are used each year to implement the LROC through issuance of the AOP, which section 602(b) of the 1968 Colorado River Basin Project Act requires as a single integrated reference on past and anticipated operations. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-001-13`, `EU-LP-OFF-022-04`.
2. **projection and tier setting** — Projected monthly inflows are entered into the 24-Month Study, starting from projected storage conditions, and releases are adjusted to accomplish project purposes; in CRSS the operating tier is set in January from the end-of-calendar-year Lake Powell elevation (for example, 3,525-3,575 ft gives the Mid-Elevation Release Tier). Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-022-14`, `EU-LP-OFF-021-09`.
3. **equalization condition** — Equalization releases require Lake Powell above the elevation in the Equalization Elevation Table, with the 2004 interim 602(a) storage guideline setting 3,630 ft (about 14.85 maf); if that elevation is reached and Lake Mead is projected below 1,105 ft on September 30, additional water is released to Lake Mead. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-019-08`, `EU-LP-OFF-001-08`.
4. **in-year revision** — In the Equalization and Balancing Tiers, scheduled water-year releases are adjusted each month based on forecast inflow and projected September 30 active storage at both reservoirs; the 1996 GAO description of practice likewise states that the remaining-year volume is recomputed each month of the January-July forecast season. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-011-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-012-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-010-06`.
5. **coordination** — Under the DCP Companion Agreement the Secretary must describe 24-Month Study assumptions and projected operations to the Basin States Parties before completing the April and August studies, and under LTEMP Reclamation holds monthly Glen Canyon Dam operational coordination meetings with DOI bureaus, WAPA, and Basin State liaisons. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-032-06`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-07`.

## SC-LP-004 — Population thresholds trigger an escalating sequence of humpback chub and trout actions under LTEMP

- Primary pattern: decision_process
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: How do monitored fish population estimates translate into flow and non-flow actions for humpback chub and rainbow trout below Glen Canyon Dam?
- Synthesis claim: LTEMP and the 2008 Biological Opinion define a threshold-based escalation tied to the currently accepted humpback chub population model. If the combined adult estimate for the Little Colorado River aggregation falls below 9,000 (2,000 mainstem and 7,000 in the Little Colorado River), Tier 1 conservation actions for rearing and recruitment are implemented. If those do not halt a decline and the adult estimate falls below 7,000, Tier 2 mechanical removal of nonnative fish begins, with up to six monthly trips, and stops when a predator index or recovery criteria are met. Separately, Trout Management Flows, a flow action of high releases followed by a rapid drop to 5,000-8,000 cfs, are triggered by predicted high young-of-year trout recruitment. A drop below 3,500 adults in any year was set in 2008 as a trigger for reinitiating ESA consultation.
- Scope and conditions: LTEMP Alternative D (2016 ROD) and the 2008 Biological Opinion reinitiation trigger. Thresholds are point estimates from the population model current at the time of decision; the 2008 trigger uses the ASMR model within its 95% confidence interval. Of these actions, only TMFs change dam releases; Tier 1 and Tier 2 are non-flow actions.

### Analysis chain

1. **monitoring signal** — Adult (>=200 mm) humpback chub numbers are estimated with the currently accepted population model; young-of-year trout recruitment is predicted for the trout trigger. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-003-13`, `EU-LP-OFF-006-07`.
2. **first threshold** — Tier 1 conservation actions are implemented if the combined adult estimate for the mainstem Little Colorado River aggregation and the Little Colorado River falls below 9,000 (2,000 mainstem, 7,000 tributary). Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-003-13`.
3. **escalation and stop rule** — If Tier 1 actions do not halt the decline and the adult estimate falls below 7,000, up to six monthly mechanical-removal trips are made each triggered year; removal stops when the predator index is below 60 rainbow trout/km for at least 2 years with low immigration, or when adult chub exceed 7,500 and subadult recruitment exceeds adult mortality for at least 2 years. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-003-14`.
4. **flow action for trout** — TMFs, triggered by predicted high young-of-year trout recruitment regardless of cause, use repeated cycles of sustained high flows (for example 20,000 cfs for 2 days to 1 week) followed by a rapid drop to 5,000-8,000 cfs. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-006-07`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-12`.
5. **regulatory backstop** — Reclamation and FWS defined a reinitiation-of-consultation trigger if the adult population declines significantly or, in any single year, drops below 3,500 adults within the 95% confidence interval of the ASMR model. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-012-06`.

### Evidence and source check

- Supporting EUs: `EU-LP-OFF-003-13`, `EU-LP-OFF-006-07`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-14`, `EU-LP-OFF-003-12`, `EU-LP-OFF-012-06`
- Supporting KCs: `KC-LP-OPR-18`
- Evidence depth: source_checked_with_direct_quote
- Source locators: OFF-003 (2016 LTEMP ROD) Attachment B Sections 2.2.1-2.2.3; OFF-006 (LTEMP FEIS Appendix Q) response 3.6; OFF-012 (2008 BiOp) Conservation Measures.
- Verification note: Rereading OFF-003 Section 2.2.3 supplied the Tier 2 start condition not captured in EU-LP-OFF-003-14: 'Mechanical removal ... will be conducted if the Tier 1 conservation actions described in the previous section were not successful in halting a decline in the number of adult humpback chub ... if the point estimate of adult humpback chub falls below 7,000'. Section 2.2.2 confirmed the 9,000 (2,000 + 7,000) Tier 1 threshold. OFF-006 confirmed TMFs are 'triggered by the prediction of high recruitment of young-of-year trout regardless of the cause'.

### Limits and implication

- Uncertainty or exception: The evidence does not report whether Tier 1, Tier 2, or TMF actions have actually been triggered since 2016. The 2008 reinitiation trigger predates LTEMP; whether it was carried into the 2016 Biological Opinion is not stated in the EUs. TMF triggers were to be developed in consultation with AZGFD and stakeholders.
- Operational implication: Most humpback chub responses are non-flow actions; a question about whether fish status changes dam releases should point to TMFs and experimental flows rather than to the Tier 1 and Tier 2 thresholds.
- Next step: Add GCMRC annual reports to document which triggers have been reached since 2016.
- Confidence: high

## SC-LP-005 — Adaptive management advises; the Secretary of the Interior decides

- Primary pattern: decision_process
- Secondary lenses: none
- Operational question: Who decides how Glen Canyon Dam operations and experiments are carried out, and what roles do the adaptive management program and decision analysis play?
- Synthesis claim: Authority over Glen Canyon Dam sub-annual operations and experiments rests with the Secretary of the Interior, while the surrounding institutions are advisory. The Adaptive Management Work Group, a federal advisory committee of agencies, Basin States, Tribes, power customers, and environmental and recreation groups, evaluates dam operations and recommends changes; the LTEMP ROD states that the Secretary retains sole discretion to decide how to accomplish operations and experiments each year. In selecting LTEMP, structured decision analysis informed but did not determine the preferred alternative, and DOI chose the term 'decision analysis' specifically because the Secretary retains decision authority.
- Scope and conditions: Governance of Glen Canyon Dam operations under the Grand Canyon Protection Act and the LTEMP ROD. Annual release volumes are set under the LROC and Interim Guidelines, which have their own consultation processes with Basin States and are not decided through the AMWG.

### Analysis chain

1. **advisory body** — The Adaptive Management Program is a federal advisory committee that evaluates the performance of dam operations and non-flow treatments using monitoring and research and makes recommendations to DOI; its work group includes federal and state agencies, the seven Basin States, Tribes, hydropower contractors, and environmental, conservation, and recreation groups. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-014-01`, `EU-LP-OFF-004-06`, `EU-LP-OFF-011-12`.
2. **decision authority** — The Secretary of the Interior retains sole discretion to decide how best to accomplish operations and experiments in any given year pursuant to the ROD and other binding obligations. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-003-16`.
3. **role of analysis tools** — Structured decision analysis informed the joint leads' analysis but was not the only method used to identify the preferred alternative; DOI used the term 'decision analysis' because the Secretary retains responsibility and authority to decide. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-006-09`, `EU-LP-RES-024-18`.
4. **routine coordination** — Reclamation seeks consensus on the annual hydrograph through monthly operational coordination calls and regular GCDAMP technical and work group meetings, and must notify Traditionally Associated Tribes at least 30 days before planned experimental flows. ## SC-LP-006 — Below minimum power pool, physical capacity, cavitation limits, and outlet reliability jointly cap Glen Canyon Dam releases

- Primary pattern: constraint_structure
- Secondary lenses: operational_tradeoff
- Operational question: What constraints determine how much water Glen Canyon Dam can release if Lake Powell falls below minimum power pool (3,490 ft)?
- Synthesis claim: Below 3,490 ft the penstocks can no longer pass water and all releases must go through the four river outlet works tubes. Three constraints then stack: design capacity that falls with head (15,000 cfs at 3,490 ft; an estimated 10,200 cfs, about 7.41 maf/yr, at 3,430 ft, below the 8.23 maf LROC objective); interim per-conduit flow limits set to reduce cavitation damage; and Reclamation's 2024 conclusion that the outlet works should not be relied on as the sole release path. Because significant structural alterations would require congressional authorization, the operating response has been to keep Lake Powell above 3,490 ft, which places the burden on release reductions.
- Scope and conditions: Glen Canyon Dam low-elevation operations as documented from the original design paper (1960s) through the March 2024 Interim Operating Guidance, the 2024 LTEMP SEIS, and the 2025 Post-2026 alternatives report. The 3,430 ft / 10,200 cfs figure is a single study cited by CRS, not a Reclamation operating rule. Elevations in OFF-025 are NGVD29.

### Analysis chain

1. **physical threshold** — Minimum water surface for power operation is 3,490 ft; below it water cannot be released through the penstocks and must pass through the river outlet works, which the dam was not envisioned to rely on. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-027-03`, `EU-LP-OFF-019-05`.
2. **capacity declining with head** — The outlets were designed for 15,000 cfs at 3,490 ft (3,750 cfs per tube); one study estimated only 10,200 cfs (7.41 maf/yr) at 3,430 ft, less than the 8.23 maf/yr requirement, and post-2026 alternatives state that releases may be less than specified below 3,490 ft because of infrastructure limits. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-027-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-007-06`, `EU-LP-CRS-002-08`, `EU-LP-OFF-020-04`.
3. **cavitation and reliability limits** — Interim guidance caps flow in each conduit to minimize cavitation damage (the Draft SEIS modeled 3,150 cfs per tube, 12,600 cfs total); a 2023 inspection found the components sufficient for periodic releases but raised concern about long-term high-flow use, and Reclamation concluded it should not rely on the outlet works as the sole release path below 3,490 ft. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-025-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-07`, `EU-LP-OFF-007-04`.
4. **remaining flexibility and its allocation** — Significant alterations to the dam would require congressional authorization; Lower Basin states have criticized limiting infrastructure protection to reduced Lake Powell releases, calling it 'shortsighted' and a cause of 'major cuts' to Lower Basin deliveries, and objected that the post-2026 EIS did not address the dam's plumbing problems. Evidence: `EU-LP-CRS-001-13`, `EU-LP-CRS-001-12`, `EU-LP-MED-002-05`.

### Evidence and source check

- Supporting EUs: `EU-LP-OFF-027-03`, `EU-LP-OFF-019-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-027-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-007-06`, `EU-LP-CRS-002-08`, `EU-LP-OFF-020-04`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-02`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-05`, `EU-LP-OFF-025-07`, `EU-LP-OFF-007-04`, `EU-LP-CRS-001-13`, `EU-LP-CRS-001-12`, `EU-LP-MED-002-05`

## SC-LP-019 — Frequent HFEs increased sand volume at most monitoring sites, but not at every bar type, and campsite area still declined

- Primary pattern: operational_consequence
- Secondary lenses: operational_tradeoff
- Operational question: What sandbar and campsite outcomes have been observed downstream of Glen Canyon Dam during the period of frequent HFEs?
- Synthesis claim: Against a background of net sand erosion in most post-dam years, the period of frequent controlled floods was followed by net sand deposition at most monitoring sites: from 2004 to 2020, with seven HFEs and above-average tributary sand input in more than half of the years, 86% of long-term sites gained sand. The response differs by bar type: wide reattachment bars and upper pool deposits have stabilized under vegetation and reached their maximum size, while separation bars and undifferentiated eddy bars aggrade during HFEs but have continued to decline over time. Over 2002-2016, usable campsite area at the same sites fell 37%, with vegetation encroachment the primary cause, so sand gains have not translated into recreational campsite area.
- Scope and conditions: Observed outcomes at the 45 long-term sandbar monitoring sites in Marble and Grand Canyons, mostly 1990-2020, as reported by USGS and the LTEMP EIS. The 2004-2020 result reflects HFEs together with tributary sand supply and other flow conditions; the sources do not attribute the gains to HFEs alone.

### Analysis chain

1. **baseline condition** — The dam cut off about 95% of the historical sediment supply, and from 1964 to 2017 net sand erosion occurred in about 69% of years in Marble Canyon and 52% in Grand Canyon. Evidence: `EU-LP-OFF-007-21`.
2. **operation** — Since 2012 HFEs have followed a protocol timed to tributary sand inputs; sandbars were larger at 52%, 52%, and 57% of monitoring sites immediately after the 2012, 2013, and 2014 HFEs. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-018-01`, `EU-LP-OFF-005-20`.
3. **observed aggregate outcome** — From 2004 to 2020, a period with seven controlled floods and above-average tributary sand input in more than half the years, net deposition occurred at 86% of long-term monitoring sites. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-018-11`.
4. **outcome by bar type** — At vegetated, stage-limited bar types HFEs can do no better than replace sediment eroded between events as accommodation space diminishes, while HFEs have not changed the long-term declining trajectory of undifferentiated eddy bars and separation bars. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-018-12`, `EU-LP-RES-018-13`.
5. **related outcome for recreation** — From 2002 to 2016 usable campsite area declined 37% at the same sites, with vegetation encroachment identified as the primary cause. Evidence: `EU-LP-RES-018-16`.
